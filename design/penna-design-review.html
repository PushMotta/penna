Design review 01 · 21 October 2026 · against DESIGN.md draft 2

Penna

Fan control that belongs to the shell. Every fan is named for what it cools and shows its RPM beside what it was asked for. Curves you drag and hear. When something goes wrong, the surface says which fan and why.

Omarchy 4.0.5 · Quickshell 0.3.2 · Qt 6.12.0 Displays 2 × 2560×1440 at 74.92 Hz (13.3 ms a frame) Desk Ryzen 9 5950X · RTX 3090, 2 fans, 30–100 % Data simulated, shaped on this desk's readings

What exists

The shell has no temperature or fan surface. CoolerControl serves a web UI from its root daemon. fan2go and fancontrol are config files. On this desk only the CPU, the NVMe drive and the 3090 are visible today; no motherboard fan driver is bound.

What we build

A root daemon that owns the fans, and three surfaces in the shell: a bar glyph that reports health, a popup for the daily controls, and a studio for curves. The daemon decides once a second. The surfaces never look like it.

The bar to clear

Hold 75 Hz with half of every frame to spare. Values glide, nothing jumps, nothing moves at rest. State is carried by shape and words, never by colour alone. Every surface works in the four themes below without an override.

A–D · Surfaces

Rendered in the shell's own tokens

Colours come from each theme's colors.toml as the shell reads them; urgent is the theme's red. Type is JetBrains Mono at the shell's scale. The popup and the studio are live: a simulated desk runs at the daemon's 1 s tick, and the surfaces glide between ticks.

  2. 2

  3. 3

The stepped temperature flickers whenever raw Tctl sits near a half degree. Penna's changes only when the value moves 0.6 of a degree past what is shown. Penna also runs one tick behind the raw feed, by design: it glides to each new value over the following second.

Sparklines slide, never redraw

SteppedCPU · Tctl · 60 s
PennaCPU · Tctl · 60 s

Penna's path is fixed between ticks and slides by a transform; only the newest segment grows each frame. In QML that's a Shape under a clipping parent, so no frame rebuilds geometry.

The marker rides the curve

SteppedCPU quiet
PennaCPU quiet

Penna interpolates the temperature and evaluates the curve there, carrying any hold as a smooth offset. When hysteresis holds the output, the marker leaves the curve and sits in the shaded band.

G · Decisions

Eight calls before QML

All eight recommendations were approved on 21 October 2026, and recorded in DESIGN.md §11.1.

  1. The glyph carries state; colour reinforces. Recommended, from the contrast table in F. Six glyphs, all in the installed font.
  2. The bar widget shows the glyph only by default. A temperature is a widget setting. Recommended: the right side of Omarchy's bar is icons, and the glyph already reports health.
  3. Fan rows lead with RPM; duty is second. Recommended: RPM is what you hear and what calibration measures.
  4. Full speed is a toggle until pressed again, not timed. The accent chevron in the bar is the reminder; fan-clock is reserved for timed overrides from the CLI. Recommended.
  5. Sparkline windows: 60 s in the popup, 5 minutes in the studio, on fixed per-sensor scales. Recommended: autoscaling makes lines jump.
  6. The studio has three columns at 1100 px. Below that width the sensors column becomes a tab beside the controls. Recommended.
  7. Three profiles ship: Quiet, Balanced, Render. Names are editable; up to five show as pills, more go into a dropdown. Recommended.
  8. Motion constants as in E: linear glide over one tick, text dead band of 0.6 of a step, 140 ms text ease, OutCubic for everything else. Recommended; judge them in E with Animations on and off.
